Research

Our method is adversarial. We attack real systems and real data releases to find out what they leak, then design defences against the attacks that worked.

Why Bharat

India now runs some of the world's largest digital systems: payments, health IDs, public transport, telecom. The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 sets new obligations for anyone who handles this data, but the evidence for what actually counts as anonymous in Indian data, at Indian scale, is still thin.

We want to supply that evidence: measured attacks, open tools, and practical guidance that data publishers, regulators and engineers in India can use.

Themes

Mobility and location data

Trajectories, origin–destination matrices and cellular records are routinely released after coarse “anonymisation”. We test those releases with re-identification and reconstruction attacks, and develop anonymisation that keeps data useful at the scale of millions of users.

k-scale (INFOCOM 2026), re-identifying 100K trajectories (AsiaCCS 2026)

Wireless tracking

Phones, earbuds and tags broadcast constantly. We show how Wi-Fi probe requests, MAC randomisation, BLE trackers and LoRaWAN identifiers can be linked back to a device, and build detection tools for people being tracked.

AirCatch (PoPETs 2027), StateFi (WiSec 2026)

Anonymisation that holds up

Many privacy guarantees are stated without being tested. We evaluate anonymisation and differential privacy against realistic adversaries, check the statistics behind privacy claims, and contribute to regulatory guidance on what anonymisation should mean.

Network and IoT security

We measure how connected devices and attackers behave in practice, from DNS configurations in smart homes to long-term changes in attack traffic on the web, and turn those measurements into concrete recommendations.

DNS guidelines for IoT (IEEE IoT-J 2026)

Collaborators

We work closely with researchers at Inria (France), KIT (Germany), IMDEA Networks (Spain) and Northeastern University (USA), and welcome collaborations with Indian institutions, agencies and companies that hold or publish personal data.
